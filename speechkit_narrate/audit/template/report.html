Pronunciation audit

Mispronounced words

Audit loop

The scoring rules were sealed before anything was measured. Each loop re-measures the whole narration. A change is promoted only if its score rises and the recognizer error rate does not get worse.

How it was checked

Every word tracked

Every spoken word has a time span in the narration and is scored against the book text, chapter by chapter.

Heard back by two listeners

Two offline speech recognizers transcribe the narration: NeMo CTC on everything, and Parakeet TDT to confirm. A word counts as mispronounced only if both get it wrong in most of its occurrences.

Dictionary and context

The voice's phonemes are compared with the CMU pronouncing dictionary for stress and sounds. Words like “close”, “use” and “record” are checked against the role they play in their sentence.

Acoustic checks

An r lowers the third formant (F3) of the voice from about 2,800 Hz to under 2,100 Hz, which catches an r that isn't in the spelling. Fixes are tried in their real sentences before they are used.

Fixes tried before use

Each fix was voiced several times in its real sentences and heard back, alongside fresh takes of the old reading. Only fixes that did better were used.

Limits

  • Recognizers forgive stress and accent, so those come from the dictionary and acoustic checks.
  • Names often have more than one acceptable reading.
  • A human listen is still the final check.
